The problem

Farming is getting harder to plan

  1. 01

    The weather is changing

    Seasons do not repeat like before. Hotter summers and dry spells make last year's plan a guess.

  2. 02

    Water is short

    Rain alone often does not cover what a crop needs, and the nearest river or canal may be far from the field.

  3. 03

    Soil is losing fertility

    Planting the same crop year after year drains the soil and lets pests and diseases build up.

How it works

Four steps to a plan

  1. Step 1

    Open the map

    A 3D map of the mountains and valleys opens near you, or on Dushanbe. Search for your village or jump to your location.

  2. Step 2

    Draw your field

    Click each corner on the terrain. Field Shift measures the area, height and slope, and checks on OpenStreetMap that the land is not water, a village or a road.

  3. Step 3

    Read the risks

    Drought, heat, fire and disease warnings for your field, each LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH, with the NASA numbers behind them and simple advice.

  4. Step 4

    Get your plan

    The best 3 crops for the field, when to plant them, how much water they need, and a 3-year rotation that keeps the soil fertile.

What you get

Everything about one field

  • 3D field map

    Mountains in 3D with shaded slopes, height colours, rivers, lakes and 3D trees. Height, slope and the way the slope faces are measured for your field.

  • Risk warnings

    Drought, heat, fire and crop disease risk from NASA data, each with a plain sentence and what to do.

  • Crop plan

    11 crops scored for your field. The best 3, their planting window, and a 3-year rotation with a legume to feed the soil.

  • Water facts

    How much water each crop needs, how much comes from rain, the irrigation you must add, and the nearest river, canal or lake.

  • 20-year climate charts

    Rainfall, temperature and soil moisture for each of the last 20 years, with the trend and this year compared to normal.

  • Land check

    Before a field is saved, OpenStreetMap shows whether it covers water, a village, a road or other land that cannot be farmed.

  • Three languages

    The app speaks English, Russian and Tajik, and switches instantly without losing your map.

  • Your fields, private

    Fields stay in your browser, or in a private account with only a username and password. Export them as GeoJSON at any time.

Questions

Good to know

Where does the data come from?

Climate history and recent weather come from NASA POWER, fire detections from NASA FIRMS (MODIS and VIIRS), the current weather from Weatherstack, your rough starting location from IPstack, and land, water and roads from OpenStreetMap. The app server fetches the data; your browser never sees the API keys.

Is the advice final?

No. Field Shift is an advisory tool. Its risk levels and crop scores come from simple, open rules, and the disease thresholds are approximate. Confirm with a local agronomist before you decide.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. It runs in the phone's browser. The panels become sheets that slide up from the bottom, and the 3D map uses a lighter terrain detail to stay smooth.

Where are my fields saved?

Without an account, in your own browser on this device. With a free private account (only a username and password, no email), on the Field Shift server, where only you can see them. You can export your fields as a GeoJSON file at any time.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Russian and Tajik. The first visit follows your browser's language, and you can switch at any time in the top bar.
